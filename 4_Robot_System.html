4.2 — System Overview

Three Core Components

🦾
Mechanical Unit
The robot body — a series of mechanical links driven by servo motors. Each junction is a joint (axis) that enables precise motion across 6 degrees of freedom.
🖥️
Controller
Houses power supply, control circuitry, and memory. Available in A-Size, B-Size, and Mate styles. Directs operation, motion, and external communication.
💾
FANUC Software
HandlingTool application software defines robot function, contains diagnostics, and communication protocols. Two versions: LR HandlingTool and HandlingTool.

4.3.1–4.3.3 — Drive System

Motors, Brakes & Encoders

FANUC AC SERVO MOTOR — LIVE SIMULATION

PWM SIGNAL
  • FANUC AC Servo Motor Each axis driven by a permanent magnet rotor motor. PWM signal delivered by the servo amplifier controls speed and torque precisely.
  • Electromagnetic Brake (90VDC) Applied mechanically (power OFF) — spring force locks the shaft. Released electrically (power ON) — armature pulled by electromagnet. Brake failure is usually from lack of current, not mechanical failure.
  • Serial Pulse Coder (SPC) FANUC proprietary optical encoder on each motor. Tracks position and speed via serial data packets. Pulse counts stored in memory and at the SPC, backed by batteries at robot base.

4.4 — Controller

Controller Components

01
Circuit Breaker
Protects equipment from electrical faults and abnormal input power causing high current. Always present on the input side.
02
E-Stop Unit
Controls the emergency stop system. Manages safety-relevant signal interfaces and external on/off signals.
03
Power Supply Unit
Converts AC input power to various DC voltage levels required by controller components and motors.
04
Main Board
Contains microprocessor, peripheral circuits, memory, and operator panel control circuit. Main CPU controls servo positioning.
05
6-Axis Servo Amplifier
Controls servomotor drive, Pulsecoder signals, brake control, EE Connector, overtravel limits, and hand broken detection.
AUX
Auxiliary Control Panel
Optional panel housing extra hardware — such as additional servo amplifiers for extended axes or additional motion groups.
⚡
DANGER: Lethal voltage is ALWAYS present at the input side of the circuit breaker whenever the controller is connected to a power source. DO NOT perform controller maintenance without proper training.

Coordinated Interpolation Groups
  • What is a Motion Group? A group of axes working together — analogous to how shoulder, elbow, wrist, and fingers all cooperate to move a human arm as one coordinated unit.
  • Extended Axes Axes beyond the standard robot axes — used for rotary axes, sliding rails, line tracking, or opening devices. Adds E1, E2, E3 position fields. Cannot be used independently of their motion group.

4.6 — Controller Startup

Cold Start Procedure

1
Visual Inspection

Inspect the robot, controller, workcell, and surrounding area. Confirm all safeguards are in place and the work envelope is clear of personnel. DO NOT power on if any hazard is found.

2
Turn On Circuit Breaker

Turn the power disconnect circuit breaker or power switch on the operator panel to ON. This applies power to the controller and robot.

3
Cold Start Initializes

Controller initializes system variables, I/O configuration, and displays the HMI screen upon successful startup. The R-30iB Plus shows the home screen with Setup, Teach, Run, and Utility options.

ALT
Cycle Power via iPendant

Safer method: Press FCTN → Next → Cycle Power → Cold → YES. Built-in delay allows residual power to dissipate before restart — minimizes risk of PCB damage. Not available on Mate controllers.

↓
Power Down

If motion is executing, press HOLD first. Then press ON/OFF (if applicable), then turn the breaker OFF. Note: changes are auto-saved to robot memory — no special save procedure required.
